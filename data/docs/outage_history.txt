Nimbus Cloud Storage Outage History

On March 14th, Nimbus experienced a 47 minute outage affecting file uploads
and downloads for all customers in the US-East region. The root cause was
identified as a misconfigured database failover: a scheduled maintenance
task triggered an automatic failover to a replica database that had not yet
finished syncing recent writes, causing a spike in query errors. The
incident was resolved by manually failing back to the primary database and
pausing automated failover triggers until the replica sync process was
fixed. A postmortem was published on the status page within 5 business
days, as required by the Enterprise SLA for Critical incidents.

On July 2nd, a shorter outage of 12 minutes affected only the search
endpoint. The cause was an unoptimized index rebuild job that consumed
excessive memory on the search cluster, causing several nodes to crash
simultaneously. Search functionality degraded to returning stale results
for roughly 20 minutes after the initial crash while the cluster recovered,
though file upload and download were unaffected throughout the incident.

On September 30th, a configuration change intended to tighten rate limiting
was accidentally deployed with limits set 10 times stricter than intended,
causing widespread 429 errors for API customers for approximately 18
minutes before the change was rolled back. This incident led to the
introduction of a mandatory staged rollout process for all rate limit
configuration changes, where new limits are first applied to 1% of traffic
and monitored for 15 minutes before wider deployment.

Following the March incident, Nimbus invested in additional failover
testing infrastructure and now runs automated failover drills monthly in a
staging environment that mirrors production traffic patterns. No Critical
severity incidents have occurred since the September 30th event. Full
incident postmortems, including timelines and remediation steps, remain
publicly available on the Nimbus status page for 18 months after
resolution.
